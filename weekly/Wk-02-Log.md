This week, I focused on companies without CIK mappings. CIKs are used to link stocks to SEC financial records, but the current lookup table is based on the current list, so it's necessary to consider whether it carries future information. Some companies currently lack CIKs, possibly due to delisting, acquisition, or name change. If we fill in the median and add a missing value flag, the model could learn which companies later disappeared from the list—information unknown at the time of prediction. Therefore, this time, a uniform rule was used to exclude companies without CIK mappings at each historical date. However, this exclusion also has limitations. Of the 499 companies in January 2010, only 338 had CIKs, meaning 161 were excluded, approximately 32%. This is a significant proportion, potentially biasing the historical sample towards companies that still exist today, so we cannot assume that exclusion eliminates survivorship bias.

To clarify this issue, I retained the original stock list and statistically analyzed the number and percentage of exclusions monthly. For companies that might have changed names, I first verified if they were the same company before deciding whether to add the mapping, avoiding concatenating data from different companies. In week 7, I compared the earlier periods with higher exclusion rates with the later periods with lower rates. If the two sets of results differed significantly, further investigation was needed to determine if the exclusion rules affected the results; the difference couldn't be directly attributed to the model. This comparison only provided clues and couldn't prove the cause on its own.

This experience made me realize that when dealing with missing data, it's crucial to first understand why the data is missing before considering how the exclusion rules might alter the research sample.
